## READING THE NUMBERS

**Geometry and visibility: the cue works.** At every sun elevation ≥ 10°, in
every azimuth except sun-dead-ahead, the shadow is inside the chase frame well
above 2 m (entry 4–20 m cross-sun; from the full 50 m with the sun astern),
and it closes on the aircraft at 3–43 deg/s ≈ 50–900 px/s over the last 5 m —
far above any perceptual threshold. **The ledger's pass bar ("on-screen and
closing readably WELL ABOVE 2 m") is MET** for elevation ≥ 10°, with one named
exception: landing straight INTO a low sun (az 0, elev ≤ 15°) the shadow only
enters the frame at 2.1–5.4 m — too late. That is a real-world truth too; the
mitigation is approach direction, not code.

**The 4× penumbra correction does not kill it — it shapes it.** With the
shipped 1.40° sun the shadow is a smudge beyond ~400 m slant (penumbra ≥ span)
and its EDGES only crisp inside ~98 m (penumbra ≤ chord). At flare heights the
penumbra is 0.05–0.58 m against a 10.1 m span (span/pen 17–200): a sharp
object exactly when it matters. The consult's "resolves as a distinct object
from several hundred metres" stays WRONG; "crisps as you descend IS the
altitude cue" is CONFIRMED numerically.

**The moving sun does not corrupt the flare cue.** The standing lesson (the
still case certifies nothing about the moving one) was probed directly: sun
motion alone moves the shadow across the ground FASTER than the descent does
when high and low-sun (15.6 vs 9.6 m/s at h=50, elev 15°), but by flare height
the contamination is < 0.7 m/s against a 2.6–9.6 m/s descent closure at every
elevation ≥ 15°. Dynamic full approaches against the live `sun_dir(t)` confirm:
closure 3.6–13.1 deg/s in the last 5 m, no frame dropouts.

**Availability is the killer, exactly as feared — now with numbers.**
- The rider is EXACTLY on the celestial equator: **50.0% of all time is
  night**, every day, every season.
- Sun above 15° (the flare-cue bar): **41.3% of time; the longest continuous
  window is 125.8 s** (mean 123.9 s), 12 windows per year (one per 5-min day).
- Sun above 45° (shadow close under the aircraft): 23.6%, windows of ~70–75 s.
- The sun climbs/sinks at **1.11–1.19 deg/s** — it moves ~23° of sky during a
  single 19.5 s final, and a 60–90 s pattern+approach spans 72–108° of sun
  motion. A 90 s approach fits inside the >15° window ONLY if begun within
  ~35 s of the sun crossing 15° rising.

## VERDICT

**YES — the cue can exist, and it is geometrically excellent when the sun is
up: but at `day_period_s = 300` it is an INTERMITTENT instrument, usable in
~125-second windows twelve times an hour, and stone dead half of all time.**
No shadow implementation changes that; it is a property of the world.

**The dial, as a number (Chad's to turn, not ours):** the usable window scales
linearly with the day length — longest >15° window = **0.419 × day_period_s**.

| wanted continuous >15° window | day_period_s needed |
|---|---|
| 180 s (one unhurried 90 s approach + margin) | **429 s (~7 min)** |
| 300 s (a couple of circuits) | **715 s (~12 min)** |
| 600 s (a session's worth) | **1431 s (~24 min)** |

The alternatives if the 5-minute day stays: accept the intermittent instrument
(it IS honest — real pilots lose the sun too), or give the shadow a non-sun
fallback (e.g. a moon shadow at `moon_intensity` nights — not probed here, and
a new mechanism, not this row).
