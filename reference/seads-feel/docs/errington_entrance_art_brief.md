# Errington Mine — Entrance Art Brief (historical reference for the Blender hero asset)

Scope: research-only reference for rebuilding the ERRINGTON MINE tunnel mouth as a history-true
hero asset. DOCS ONLY — no code touched. Every factual claim carries an inline source. Claims are
tagged **[ERR-DOC]** (documented for Errington itself) or **[ERA]** (typical of Sudbury-basin
mines of the same period, used as analogue where Errington photos are scarce).

## HISTORY (short)

- **What it actually was — CORRECTION for the fiction.** Errington was **not** a Mond nickel mine.
  It was a **base-metal (copper–zinc–lead–silver–gold) sulphide mine** in the *sedimentary Vermilion
  Formation* on the SW rim of the Sudbury Basin, Creighton–Davies Township, ~18 mi (29 km) from
  Sudbury. **[ERR-DOC]** (https://www.geologyontario.mndm.gov.on.ca/mndmfiles/mdi/data/records/MDI41I11SW00005.html ;
  https://www.mindat.org/loc-203947.html)
- **Discovery → option.** Base-metal mineralization near here traces to James Stobie (1897) at Stobie
  Falls on the **Vermilion River**, ½ mi west of the later Errington workings; Alphonse Ollier later
  found massive pyrite. In **1924** T. Lindsley and **Joseph Errington** optioned the ground and turned
  it to the **Treadwell Yukon Company Ltd.** **[ERR-DOC]** (https://www.onemine.org/documents/errington-and-vermilion-lake-mines)
- **Operator & dates.** **Treadwell Yukon Co. Ltd., 1924–1931** (No. 1 shaft sunk 1926). Development:
  ~26,790 ft of lateral work, ~75,500 ft of diamond drilling, **three shafts** — No. 1 to the 500-ft
  level, **No. 2 to 1,500 ft** (the deep production shaft), No. 3 = 409 ft. A **pilot mill / concentrator
  and an entire company village** were built. All work **ceased 1931** with the collapse in metal prices.
  **[ERR-DOC]** (onemine, above; MDI record, above)
- **Pilot-mill run.** 186,172 tons milled averaging 1.07% Cu, 1.10% Pb, 4.60% Zn, 0.03 oz/t Au,
  1.70 oz/t Ag. **[ERR-DOC]** (onemine, above)
- **Later life (context only, out of game era).** Re-explored 1952–54 (Ontario Pyrites Ltd.), held
  1954–60 by Consolidated Sudbury Basin Mines Ltd., folded into Giant Yellowknife Mines 1960; a new
  1,000-ton/day concentrator was built ~1957 but **never operated**. **[ERR-DOC]** (MDI record, above)
- **Why it matters for the game.** The real home base is a **1920s boomtown-then-ghost**: three
  timbered shafts, a mill, and a purpose-built village that died almost overnight in the 1931 price
  crash. That "built fast, abandoned mid-stride" character is the emotional note the entrance should
  carry. Note: **"Errington Mine No. 3"** in the fiction = the **No. 3 shaft (409 ft)**, not a separate mine.

## THE LOOK (early-20th-century surface plant)

No richly detailed surface description survives in the public sources for Errington specifically, so
the inventory below is anchored on the ONE confirmed Errington surface photo (the mill) plus **[ERA]**
analogues from contemporaneous Sudbury / Northern-Ontario mines. Treat silhouettes as reliable and
exact dimensions as **inferred** unless tagged [ERR-DOC].

- **Headframe (the landmark).** Sudbury/Ontario headframes of 1905–1928 were **rectangular timbered
  "gallows/gallows-frame" towers**, later ones sheathed. Small timber two-post gallows ran ~25 ft;
  substantial contemporary Ontario timber headframes reached far higher — the McIntyre No. 11 (1924)
  was **~175 ft, tongue-and-groove plank sheathed and clad in galvanized (corrugated) iron**; a steel
  inclined-shaft frame of the era rose ~148 ft. For Errington's mid-size shafts assume a **timber
  frame roughly 20–30 m (65–100 ft) tall**, likely part-clad in corrugated iron. **[ERA]**
  (https://www.timminstoday.com/column/remember-this/the-headframe-that-became-a-local-landmark-and-how-it-happened-1441380 ;
  https://sah-archipedia.org/buildings/MI-01-HO16.2 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Headframe)
- **Inclined shafts.** Most early Sudbury shafts were **inclined (~60° from horizontal)**, so the
  headframe leans and the hoist ropes/**skipways** exit the collar at an angle and climb the frame's
  back — a distinctive raking-brace silhouette rather than a plumb tower. **[ERA]**
  (Creighton #2, https://djoyceminerals.com/sudbury-mining-district-history-geology-and-mineralogy/)
- **Combination headframe + rockhouse.** Early Sudbury practice fused the headframe with a taller
  **rockhouse** behind/above it: ore skipped to the top, dumped, screened/sorted, and dropped to ore
  bins / rail below. Twin skips counterbalanced (one up loaded, one down empty). Expect a **stepped,
  boxy timber massing** climbing beside the frame. **[ERA]** (djoyceminerals, above)
- **Mill / concentrator building (Errington's confirmed structure).** LAC photo PA-014075 shows the
  **"Mill Building, Errington Mine, Treadwell Yukon Co."** — a large gable-roofed industrial shed that
  steps DOWN the hillside in tiers (gravity-flow milling), long axis into the slope. Timber-frame,
  corrugated-iron or board clad, multiple stove/vent stacks. Footprint on the order of a **40–60 m ×
  15–20 m** stepped shed. **[ERR-DOC silhouette]** (https://recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/home/record?IdNumber=3377055&app=FonAndCol)
- **Boiler / power house.** A separate Errington photo (early 1934, snow on ground) shows the
  **headframe with a boiler house** — low, long, with a tall smokestack for steam hoisting/heating
  plant. **[ERR-DOC]** (Geraldton Public Library print, cited via onemine, above)
- **Ore cars / tramway / trestle.** Ore and waste moved on **narrow-gauge rail in small tip cars**,
  run out over **timber trestles** to dump waste rock; a rail spur/tram tied shaft → rockhouse → mill.
  **[ERA]** (djoyceminerals, above; general Sudbury practice)
- **Rock dumps.** Angular grey **waste-rock (quartzite/carbonate) dumps** spilling from trestle ends,
  fanning downslope — a defining ground texture. **[ERR-DOC ore type: quartzite–carbonate host]** (MDI record, above)
- **Roast yards (nearby, atmospheric only).** Before 1929 Sudbury ores were open-**roasted on giant
  cordwood beds "up to 2 km long"** with sulphurous smoke killing surrounding vegetation — the source
  of the region's blackened, barren rock look. Errington was a base-metal mill, not a roaster, but the
  scorched-rock palette is period-correct. **[ERA]** (djoyceminerals, above)
- **Materials / color.** **Weathered silvered timber, corrugated galvanized iron (rusting to streaky
  browns/greys), tarpaper roofs, bare blast-fractured grey-black rock, ochre/grey rock dumps, soot.**
  A near-monochrome palette even in life — ideal for the 1940s B&W art direction. **[ERA]**

## BLENDER BLOCKOUT SPEC (hero entrance, stylized-oversize bore)

Scene target: a **220 m wide × 180 m tall bore mouth** in an **open-cut pit**. The bore is deliberately
giant vs history — resolve the scale tension by **clustering believable human-scale structures on the
FLANKS of the mouth** so the eye reads the bore as a stylized exaggeration of a real ~5 m portal.
**Do NOT straddle the bore with the headframe.** Whole ensemble **< 40k tris**; raylib-friendly flat
greyscale materials, no PBR textures.

Element list (build these):
1. **Portal timber sets — the mouth frame.** Stacked square-timber "sets" (caps + posts, ~30 cm
   timbers) ringing the LOWER, human-scale lip of the bore where structures meet rock: 6–10 receding
   sets fading into dark. Reads as the true adit the giant bore "grew from." ~6–8k tris. [ERA portal joinery]
2. **Headframe landmark — pit rim, flanking, NOT over the bore.** One timber inclined-shaft
   **gallows headframe ~24 m tall** on the pit's upper flank as a skyline landmark; raking back-braces,
   sheave silhouette at top, part corrugated-iron clad. ~4–6k tris. (Massing from ★2/★3.)
3. **Rockhouse / mill ruin — one hero building.** Tiered gable shed stepping down the pit wall
   (massing from ★1), partly collapsed/open-framed to sell "abandoned mid-stride." ~8–10k tris.
4. **Ore-car trestle.** Timber trestle running from rockhouse toward the bore lip, a couple of tipped
   ore cars, ending in a **rock-dump fan**. ~4k tris.
5. **Conveyor / skipway line.** A single raking covered gallery (box beam on trestle bents) linking
   shaft collar → rockhouse, echoing the inclined skipway. ~3k tris.
6. **Boiler house + stack.** Low long shed with a tall thin smokestack near the headframe (from ★2)
   — vertical accent against the horizontal pit. ~2–3k tris.
7. **Ground dressing.** Angular waste-rock dumps, a few timber stacks, scattered rail. Instanced
   low-poly rock, ~3–4k tris budget.

Scale treatment: keep buildings 5–25 m (real), pin them to the flanks/rim; let the void of the bore do
the awe. Portal timber sets bridge the two scales at the lip.

Material / art direction (1940s B&W photo look):
- **Flat greyscale ramp**: near-black fractured rock, mid-grey weathered timber, light-grey corrugated
  iron, white-grey sky-lit edges. 4–6 flat vertex/material greys, no color.
- **Read via silhouette + value**, not texture: strong AO in the bore, rim-light on the headframe,
  soot-darkened lower structures. Optional faint film-grain/vignette in-engine, not in the mesh.
- One warm exception permitted for gameplay: lamp/portal glow at the mouth (the game already uses
  DrawSphere/DrawCylinderEx glow primitives, per project notes) — keep the *structures* monochrome.

## OPEN QUESTIONS for Chad

1. **Fiction vs history — the nickel premise.** History says Errington was a **Treadwell Yukon
   copper-zinc-lead-silver-gold** mine (1924–31), *not* a Mond nickel mine. Keep the game's
   nickel/Mond framing, or re-fictionalize the base as the real 1920s base-metal Treadwell camp?
2. **Headframe: timber vs sheathed.** Era ran both bare-timber gallows and iron-clad towers. Bare
   timber = more skeletal/haunting; corrugated-iron clad = bulkier/industrial. Which silhouette?
3. **Inclined vs vertical frame.** Real early Sudbury shafts were **inclined ~60°** (leaning frame,
   raking skipway) — visually richer but less "iconic tower." Lean into the incline, or use an
   upright frame for readability?
4. **State: working vs ghost.** Depict the plant **operating (1926–28)** or **abandoned/ruined
   (post-1931)**? The ruin reads better with the game's ghost-town tunnel fiction, but a live plant
   gives smoke/lights.
5. **Which shaft is the "mouth"?** No. 2 (deep, 1,500 ft) is the natural hero; No. 3 (409 ft) matches
   the "No. 3" name. Pick the shaft the giant bore represents so the landmark headframe reads as *its* frame.
6. **Roast-yard atmosphere.** Include a distant blackened cordwood roast-bed field for period mood,
   even though Errington itself milled rather than roasted? (Historically adjacent, not on-site.)
